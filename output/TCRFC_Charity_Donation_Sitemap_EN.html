Every partner venue gets a URL of its own, which is what makes the donations it brings in measurable and its rebate calculable. The admin produces PNG, SVG and a print-ready PDF carrying the venue name.

  • ✓The URL cannot be derived from a venue numberSo nobody can guess another venue’s address. The URL also carries no amount or share percentage, so there is nothing worth tampering with.
  • ✓Error correction level Q (25%), code area at least 3×3 cmPrinted material picks up grease and partial cover; leave the correction and the quiet zone.
  • ✓Only the three approved logo lockups may be usedNo custom typesetting, no SINCE, no year.
  • !Reissuing a URL kills the old QR code immediatelyThe admin warns explicitly and records who did it; cards already printed have to be collected.

The first thing a customer sees. What matters most is that they recognise the place they are sitting in before choosing a project — which is why the venue name sits at the top.

  • 1Venue identityThe name is required; a logo or photo is optional. With no logo it falls back to a plain text name block rather than leaving an empty frame.

  • 3Project card wallCover image, project name, one-line description, and a "read more and donate" button. No fundraising progress.
  • 4Trust blockA club introduction, registration and tax ID details, and a link back to the charity record on the club site.
  • 5FooterPrivacy policy, donation terms, contact.
Two behaviours to note
If the partnership has ended, the URL still opens; it simply drops the venue block and behaves as the general entry — cards already printed never become dead links.
The first screen must not depend on a large image, so it opens fast on mobile data.

Invoices, use of funds

This page deliberately puts the donation form last: explain the project, be explicit about where the money goes, and let the reader decide at the end. The order is part of the design. There is no fundraising progress — the donor sees what the project does, not how far short it is.

  • 1Cover and titleProject name and a one-line appeal.
  • 2Project descriptionLaid out in the admin with a block editor: rich text, images, pull quotes, lists.
  • 3Use of fundsAn explicit statement of where the money goes.
  • 4Linked charity programmeOptional. Links to the matching programme page on the club site so donors can see past results.
  • 5Donation formAmount chips are set per project in the admin, along with a minimum and maximum per donation. Name and email are required, with the email format validated as it is typed. The button states the actual amount, so nobody taps it by accident.
  • 6FAQInvoices, use of funds.
How venue attribution survives the journey
Arriving from the landing page, the URL carries the venue code and it is also written to a short-lived 24-hour cookie. The pink line mid-page — "you are donating via Haowei Diner" — tells the donor about the attribution without overshadowing the project.
If the venue genuinely cannot be resolved, the donation still goes through: it is recorded as having no venue, the venue rebate is zero, and the flow is never interrupted.

Three states share one address: complete, not completed, and processing while the payment is still being confirmed.

  • 1CompleteThanks, order number, amount, project, the status of the invoice or receipt, a share button and a way back.
  • 2Invoice status is on the pageSo the donor does not have to go hunting in their inbox. The email address is masked; no full personal data is displayed.
  • 3Not completedLikely reasons, a retry button, and contact details.
  • !Processing must not spin foreverIt polls automatically, but the timeout and the copy after it must be defined in advance — and the copy must never read as failure, or people pay twice.
